# Coding review: Holder v. Humanitarian Law Project, 561 U.S. 1 (2010)

Reviewed against the full majority opinion from
`static.case.law`. The CAP text carries no star pagination, so quotations are
verbatim but unpinned; a reviewer confirming this coding should re-cite from the
reporter.

**Status: still `model_draft`.** A model read the opinion and checked each axis
against it. That is not what `model_verified` asserts, which is that a human did.

## Disposition → `outcome: restricted`

> We conclude that the material-support statute is constitutional as applied to
> the particular activities plaintiffs have told us they wish to pursue. We do
> not, however, address the resolution of more difficult cases that may arise
> under the statute in the future.

The "as applied" qualifier matters for a corpus of this kind: the holding is
narrow by its own terms.

## `restriction_type: content_based`

> § 2339B regulates speech on the basis of its content. Plaintiffs want to speak
> to the PKK and LTTE, and whether they may do so under § 2339B depends on what
> they say.

The Court expressly rejected the Government's O'Brien framing, so this is the
opinion's own characterisation and not an inference.

## `harm_proximity: foreseeable`

> The Government, when seeking to prevent imminent harms in the context of
> international affairs and national security, is not required to conclusively
> link all the pieces in the puzzle before we grant weight to its empirical
> conclusions.

CHECK: the Court used "imminent" while deferring rather than finding. `foreseeable`
reads that as deference to a predicted harm, which is a judgement a human should
confirm — `imminent` is arguable on the same sentence.

## `capability_tier: undetermined`

Training and expert advice are a means of doing something, so the axis applies;
the Court did not grade it.

## Axes the opinion supports

`distribution_modality: training_code` — CHECK, the "artifact" was training and
expert advice, not code; this is the corpus's loosest analogy after Sorrell.
`actor_type: foreign_state_entity` — CHECK, the *plaintiffs* were a US
non-profit and US citizens; the designated organisations were the recipients.
The axis is coding the recipient, which no other record does.
`forum: private_transfer`, `jurisdiction: us_federal`,
`post_release_modification: none`.
