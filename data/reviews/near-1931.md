# Coding review: Near v. Minnesota ex rel. Olson, 283 U.S. 697 (1931)

Reviewed against the full majority opinion from
`static.case.law`. The CAP text carries no star pagination, so quotations are
verbatim but unpinned; a reviewer confirming this coding should re-cite from the
reporter.

**Status: still `model_draft`.** A model read the opinion and checked each axis
against it. That is not what `model_verified` asserts, which is that a human did.

## Disposition → `outcome: permitted`

> we hold the statute, so far as it authorized the proceedings in this action
> under clause (b) of section one, to be an infringement of the liberty of the
> press guaranteed by the Fourteenth Amendment.

## `restriction_type: content_based` — kept, but anachronistic

The statute reached any

> malicious, scandalous and defamatory newspaper, magazine or other periodical

which sorts publications by what they say. But 1931 predates the
content-based/content-neutral framework entirely — the opinion contains **zero**
occurrences of either term, or of "intermediate scrutiny", or of O'Brien. The
value describes the statute in vocabulary the Court did not have. CHECK: a human
should decide whether that is an acceptable translation or an imposition.

## `harm_proximity: undetermined`

The Court struck the statute on its face and made no finding about what The
Saturday Press would cause. Its famous exceptions — troop movements, obscenity,
incitement — are dicta about other cases.

## `capability_tier: inapplicable`

A scandal sheet conveys allegations, not a means of doing anything. Nothing to
grade, as distinct from ungraded.

## Axes the opinion supports

`distribution_modality: paper`, `actor_type: firm` — CHECK, Near and Guilford
were individuals publishing a periodical — `forum: journal`,
`jurisdiction: us_state` (a Minnesota statute, reviewed on Fourteenth Amendment
grounds), `post_release_modification: none`.
